How to read this scatter plot Each dot is one U.S. state. The X-axis shows the state minimum wage for the selected year. The Y-axis shows the ACS outcome you've chosen (e.g., poverty rate).

Trendline = OLS regression (least-squares fit) A downward-sloping trendline means states with higher minimum wages tend to have lower poverty rates. This is a correlation, not causation — many other factors differ between states.

04 Kaitz Index & Correlation Matrix How far the minimum wage reaches into the distribution
Kaitz Index by State
Min wage as % of estimated median wage · 2024
ℹ
What is the Kaitz Index? The Kaitz Index measures how high the minimum wage is relative to the typical wage in a state — expressed as a percentage. Kaitz = Min Wage / Median Individual Wage × 100 A Kaitz of 50% means the minimum wage is half the median wage. A higher Kaitz means the minimum wage covers a larger share of the workforce. International comparisons: France ≈ 60%, UK ≈ 55%, U.S. federal ≈ 25%.

Here, median individual wage is estimated as (ACS median household income × 0.55) ÷ 2,080 annual hours. Colors: red > 55% (binding), green 40–55% (moderate), grey < 40% (non-binding).
Median individual wage estimated from ACS median HH income × 0.55 ÷ 2,080 hrs. Lower estimate — actual binding effect may be stronger.

How to read the correlation matrix Each cell shows the Pearson r between the row variable and the column variable across all states. r ranges from −1 (perfect inverse) to +1 (perfect direct) Color guide:
🔵 Blue = positive r (both variables rise together)
🔴 Red = negative r (one rises as the other falls)
⬜ White ≈ no linear relationship

Example: if Min Wage × Poverty shows r = −0.45 (red), states with higher minimums tend to have lower poverty rates on average.

05 Lorenz Curve & Gini Coefficient Income inequality + where the minimum wage sits
📐
How to read the Lorenz Curve & Gini Coefficient
The Lorenz curve plots cumulative income share (Y) against cumulative population share (X), sorted from poorest to richest. The line of perfect equality (diagonal) is where everyone has the same income. The further the Lorenz curve bows below the diagonal, the more unequal the distribution. The Gini coefficient is twice the area between the diagonal and the Lorenz curve — it ranges from 0 (perfect equality) to 1 (one person has everything). The vertical red line marks where full-time minimum wage workers fall in the income distribution — showing how far up (or down) the min wage reaches.

What is the Gini Coefficient? The Gini coefficient is the most widely used measure of income inequality. It is calculated as twice the area between the line of perfect equality and the Lorenz curve. Gini = 2 × (Area between diagonal and Lorenz curve) Scale:
0.0 = Perfect equality (everyone earns the same)
0.25–0.35 = Low inequality (Scandinavia)
0.35–0.45 = Moderate (most U.S. states)
0.45–0.55 = High (D.C., NY, CA)
1.0 = Perfect inequality

U.S. national Gini ≈ 0.49 (2023, Census Bureau). Calculated here from ACS income quintile shares.
